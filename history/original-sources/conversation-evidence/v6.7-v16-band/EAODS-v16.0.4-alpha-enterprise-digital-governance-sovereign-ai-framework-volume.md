# EAODS v16.0-alpha
## Volume 5: Enterprise Identity Sovereignty, Human–AI Authority Delegation & Constitutional Trust Framework

# Purpose

The Enterprise Identity Sovereignty Framework establishes identity as a constitutional enterprise construct rather than a technical authentication mechanism.

Identity defines who may act, under what delegated authority, within which constitutional boundaries, for what duration, under which governance controls, and with what continuing accountability.

Every autonomous action shall be attributable to a governed identity.

---

# Strategic Objectives

The framework shall:

- establish sovereign enterprise identities;
- govern delegated authority;
- strengthen Domain 03 privileged identity management;
- improve accountability across autonomous systems;
- reduce unauthorized authority expansion;
- preserve constitutional traceability;
- support continuous trust evaluation.

---

# Constitutional Identity Principles

Enterprise identity shall be:

- unique;
- attributable;
- continuously verified;
- least-privileged;
- revocable;
- observable;
- evidence-supported;
- constitutionally governed.

Authority shall never exist without an associated governed identity.

Every autonomous identity shall maintain:

- supervising human authority;
- delegated mission scope;
- operational constraints;
- escalation path;
- intervention authority;
- certification status;
- assurance history.

Autonomous identities shall not self-authorize expanded authority.

# Privileged Identity Governance

Privileged identities shall require:

- enhanced authentication;
- continuous monitoring;
- session recording where appropriate;
- periodic access review;
- independent approval;
- rapid revocation capability.

Emergency privilege elevation shall require documented justification and post-event review.

- privileged cyber identities;
- incident response authorities;
- forensic authority delegation;
- threat intelligence publication rights;
- detection engineering administration;

# Constitutional Trust Attestations

Every identity shall maintain:

- trust score;
- assurance status;
- certification validity;
- policy compliance;
- supervisory approval;
- operational history.

Trust attestations shall support governance decisions but shall not replace executive judgment.

# Identity Separation of Duties

The framework shall prevent a single identity from simultaneously exercising incompatible responsibilities including:

- policy approval and assurance;
- development and production authorization;
- investigation and evidence adjudication;
- risk acceptance and independent audit.

Separation rules shall be machine-evaluable where feasible.

# Enterprise Case Study

## Scenario

A multinational aerospace organization deploys autonomous engineering, legal, cybersecurity, and executive advisory agents supporting highly regulated operations.

### Challenge

Leadership requires clear constitutional boundaries separating executive authority from delegated autonomous authority while preserving rapid operational execution.

### EAODS Implementation

The Enterprise Identity Sovereignty Framework establishes constitutionally governed identities for humans, AI agents, services, and infrastructure. Every autonomous mission references a supervising executive identity, delegated authority scope, trust attestation, and evidence chain. Domain 03 privileged cyber identities receive enhanced governance, continuous monitoring, and mandatory post-operation review.

### Outcome

The organization creates a transparent authority model where every enterprise action can be traced to an accountable constitutional identity. Identity governance becomes a strategic governance capability supporting executive confidence, cybersecurity resilience, regulatory readiness, and autonomous enterprise operations.
