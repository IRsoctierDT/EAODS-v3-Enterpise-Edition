Enterprise AI Security Operations Reference Architecture & Operating Model

Purpose

This standard establishes the Enterprise AI Security Operations Center (AI-SOC) operating model that unifies governance, threat intelligence, detection engineering, response automation, incident command, cyber recovery, resilience validation, and AI orchestration into a single enterprise operating architecture.

The AI-SOC serves as the operational realization of EAODS, providing a governed execution model where humans and AI agents collaborate through policy-driven workflows supported by continuous evidence generation and executive oversight.

⸻

Strategic Objectives

The AI-SOC shall:

* unify operational cybersecurity capabilities;
* establish standardized human and AI responsibilities;
* enable policy-driven automation;
* improve operational scalability;
* reduce response latency;
* provide executive operational visibility;
* support measurable cyber resilience.

⸻

Enterprise Operating Principles

Operations shall be:

* governance-first;
* evidence-driven;
* policy-authorized;
* human-accountable;
* continuously observable;
* AI-assisted;
* resilient by design;
* continuously improved.

Enterprise Case Study

Scenario

A multinational organization operates thousands of endpoints, multiple cloud environments, AI-enabled business systems, and globally distributed security teams. Existing operations rely on disconnected tools, duplicated processes, and inconsistent governance.

Challenge

Leadership requires a unified operating model that integrates governance, AI-assisted analysis, incident response, recovery, and continuous assurance while maintaining executive accountability.

EAODS Implementation

The Enterprise AI Security Operations Reference Architecture establishes a centralized Mission Orchestrator coordinating specialized AI agents under policy-driven governance. Security telemetry flows through the Enterprise Security Data Fabric into Detection Engineering and Threat Intelligence workflows. Response Automation, Incident Command, and Cyber Recovery execute under enterprise authorization controls, while all evidence and operational decisions are preserved within the Enterprise Knowledge Graph and visualized through the Executive Control Tower.

Outcome

The organization establishes a standardized AI-SOC capable of governed automation, measurable operational performance, improved analyst efficiency, stronger executive oversight, and continuous cyber resilience aligned with enterprise governance objectives.
